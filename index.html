★ The ½ origin shift

One shared real axis, read with two origins:

  • 0 origin of the number line (primes, counting)
  • ½ origin of the ζ-value frame (the critical line)

ζ is drawn at (½ + Re ζ, t, Im ζ): its real part is measured from the critical line, its imaginary part lives on the depth (Z) axis.

Payoff: a non-trivial zero becomes a geometric incidence — the magenta ribbon touches the critical line exactly when ζ = 0. Zeros are simply where the value-ribbon returns to the line.

Try it: drag “Origin shift” to slide the output frame 0 → 1 and watch the zeros snap onto the critical line exactly at ½. The whole output frame (XZ floor, YZ plane and its axis) slides as one rigid unit.

● Sweeping the XZ plane

Drag “XZ Grid Y” to raise or lower the floor to a height t = Im(s) on the critical line.

The red marker rides the magenta ribbon and reads off ζ(½ + it) at that height — the label shows the value as a + bi together with |ζ| and arg ζ.

A radial line runs from the marker straight to the output-frame axis. It is the value drawn as a phasor: its length is |ζ| and its direction is arg ζ. Sweep t and it rotates and pulses.

The faint phasor trail is the value curve over a window of t flattened into the floor — a flat read on the ribbon's depth (its winding) where perspective is hard. Toggle it with “Show phasor trail” and size it with “trail ± window”.

Whenever the radial line shrinks to nothing — the marker touching the axis, |ζ| → 0 — you are sitting on a non-trivial zero. At t = 0 the marker reads the real value ζ(½) ≈ −1.46.

The three grids
XY plane (Z = 0)
The complex s-plane and the counting plane. X = Re(s), Y = Im(s) = t (also the π-count height). Holds the primes, the staircase, and the zeros on the critical line.
XZ plane (movable floor)
The output-value plane at height t = “XZ Grid Y”. X = Re(ζ), Z = Im(ζ), anchored on the critical line. The red marker reads off ζ at that height.
YZ plane (output-frame origin)
The origin plane of the ζ-value frame. Its grey vertical centre line is the output-frame t-axis — the foot the radial line drops onto. It slides with “Origin shift”; at ½ it sits on the critical line, at 0 on the imaginary axis (Re = 0).
